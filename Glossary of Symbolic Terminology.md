# Glossary of Fitary Logic and Symbolic Terminology

This glossary defines key terms used throughout the fitary logic framework. All symbolic terminology, including “fit” and “fitary,” was coined by David K. Hill to support mnemonic clarity, modular encoding, and legacy-proof documentation.

---

## 🔹 Fit  
A symbolic unit representing one of fifteen possible states in base-15 logic. Coined by the author to replace binary-centric terminology with a more mnemonic and modular alternative. Fits are designed to harmonize with crystalline structures and resonance-based computation.

## 🔹 Fitary  
An adjective describing systems, gates, or structures that operate within fit-based symbolic logic. Fitary systems are modular, mnemonic, and designed for audit-friendly encoding across platforms and generations.

## 🔹 Crystalline Structure  
A symbolic and architectural metaphor used to describe the ordered, resonant arrangement of fitary states. Crystalline structures support harmonic mapping and modular logic gate design, enabling reproducible teaching and encoding.

## 🔹 Resonance Mapping  
A method of aligning symbolic states with harmonic intervals or spatial patterns. Resonance mapping is used to visualize fitary logic gates and encode mnemonic relationships between symbolic units.

## 🔹 Symbolic Gate  
A modular logic gate that operates on fitary states rather than binary inputs. Symbolic gates are designed to be teachable, auditable, and adaptable to future computing paradigms.

## 🔹 Legacy-Proofing  
The practice of designing systems, terminology, and documentation that remain understandable, reproducible, and meaningful across generations. Legacy-proofing includes timestamping, modular formatting, and symbolic clarity.

## 🔹 Mnemonic Encoding  
Encoding schemes that prioritize human memorability and symbolic intuition. Mnemonic encoding supports teaching, troubleshooting, and long-term retention of fitary logic structures.

## 🔹 Symlink-Centric Infrastructure  
A modular approach to system design that uses symbolic links to manage environments, dependencies, and documentation. Symlink-centric infrastructure supports cross-platform automation and audit-friendly workflows.

## 🔹 Static Architecture  
A deployment model where systems run directly from disk without dynamic dependencies. Static architecture supports legacy-proofing, reproducibility, and offline access.
